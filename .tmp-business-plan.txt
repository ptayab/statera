The reporting gap
On most sites today, hazards, near misses, and incidents are logged on paper forms, whiteboards, or generic systems built for the office rather than the field. A worker who spots a hazard must stop, find a form or a computer, and fill out paperwork, often at the end of a shift, if at all. The reports that do get filed then sit in binders or disconnected spreadsheets where supervisors can't easily triage them, track them to resolution, or learn from them. The result is a reporting gap where the most valuable early-warning signals, near misses and hazards, are the least likely to be captured.
Underreporting: Industry research and decades of safety practice consistently find that the majority of near misses and hazards go unreported, and those are exactly the early-warning signals that precede serious incidents. Some EHS-software studies estimate up to ~90% go unreported.
Why it happens: reporting is hard in the field (no quick tool at the face or in the pit), workers fear blame, paper and legacy systems are slow, and safety data ends up scattered across disconnected systems.
Why it matters now
Workforce shortage: MiHR's 2026 Canadian Mining Labour Market Outlook projects the industry needs about 246,000 hires through 2035 (baseline), with roughly 86% replacing retiring workers and nearly half of the need arriving by 2027. Experienced workers are leaving and being replaced by newer, less-experienced ones, which makes reliable hazard reporting and knowledge capture more critical, not less.
Cost of incidents: a single serious incident can mean downtime, regulatory fines, higher insurance premiums, and lasting reputational damage, often hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars, dwarfing the cost of prevention.
Regulatory and ESG pressure: regulators and investors increasingly judge operators on safety performance, so strong safety reporting and transparency are becoming table stakes for permits, ESG ratings, and social licence to operate.
4. Solution & Product
Overview
Statera is a safety communication and ticketing platform for frontline mine workers and their supervisors. A worker captures a hazard, near miss, or incident in seconds from a phone or tablet; each report becomes a tracked ticket that AI helps supervisors’ triage and prioritize; the platform keeps the right people informed as the issue moves to resolution; and it turns the accumulated reports into insight, surfacing recurring risks and trends before they become incidents. In short: capture → triage → communication → insight, with nothing falling through the cracks.
Key capabilities
Fast, field-friendly reporting (few taps, photo, works in tough conditions) → more hazards and near misses get captured, closing the reporting gap.
Ticketing with closed-loop tracking and communication → every issue is assigned, tracked to completion, and everyone stays informed, so nothing slips and workers see their reports acted on.
AI-assisted triage and analytics → supervisors handle a high volume of reports with less effort and catch recurring risks early, preventing incidents before they happen.
What makes it different
Statera is built for the field, not the office: reporting takes seconds in real mine conditions, so workers will report more often. It's mining-specific and pairs AI-assisted triage with closed-loop tracking, so supervisors act on more issues with less effort instead of drowning in them. And it runs entirely in a Canadian data region, meeting the residency and privacy expectations mining operator’s demand.

6. Business Model
How we charge
We land with a low-risk pilot at a single site, then convert it to an annual per-site licence and expand across the operator's other sites. Pricing is per site, not per user: every field worker can report for free, and the licence covers the supervisor and admin seats. This is a deliberate decision to make sure costs don’t discourage more reporting, which is the whole point of Statera. Each licence includes Canadian-region hosting, security, support, and ongoing updates.

We sell directly to mine operators! We will start in our own backyard and then grow by turning one proven site into many. Mining is a tight, relationship-driven industry, so our motion is built on warm introductions and reference customers.

Pilot approach
First, we will start at one site with a defined group of supervisors and field workers. We will give them our Pilot Plan for three months, with success metrics we agree on before we start:  number of reports submitted, time from hazard to action, and worker adoption. The mine owns its data, hosting stays in a Canadian region, and there's no long-term commitment. Founding partners get preferred terms in exchange for acting as a reference.
Expansion
Each successful site is the wedge. One site proves value, we roll out across that operator's other sites, then use their results as the reference that wins the next operator. The path moves outward: Saskatchewan → Western Canada → rest of Canada → sister industries (construction, metals) → Internation). In a small industry, referrals and case studies compound fast.

9. Technology, Data Security & Compliance
Architecture
Statera is a cloud-hosted web and mobile platform. Field workers submit hazard, near-miss, and incident reports from a phone or tablet; those reports flow into a central, secure database where supervisors triage, track, and analyze them through a web/phone dashboard. The AI features (report classification, prioritization, and trend detection) run as a separate service that processes report data on demand. Everything is hosted in a Canadian cloud region, so data stays in Canada from capture through storage and processing.
Data security
Encryption: all data encrypted in transit (TLS/HTTPS) and at rest (AES-256).
Access controls & audit logging: role-based access (field worker, supervisor, admin) on a least-privilege basis, with an audit log of who viewed or changed each record; optional single sign-on for enterprise customers.
Backups & recovery: automated, encrypted daily backups with point-in-time recovery and a tested restore process.
Data residency & privacy
Canadian residency: all data is stored and processed in a Canadian cloud region (e.g., AWS Canada Central, Montréal), and AI processing uses a provider that keeps data in-region and does not use it to train models.
PIPEDA-aligned handling: we collect only the worker information needed, the mine owns its data, access is restricted and logged, and we honour defined retention and deletion policies.
Responsible use
Statera assists safety reporting and communication; it supports, and does not replace, a site's own safety procedures, supervisor judgment, or regulatory obligations. AI outputs (such as prioritization and trend flags) are decision-support only and remain reviewable by a person.
